# PortugueseSpeller (pt-BR)

Brazilian Portuguese, following the norm used for amounts written out in contracts and
cheques.

| Amount | Output |
|---|---|
| 1 | um dólar |
| 25,1 | vinte e cinco dólares e dez centavos |
| 0,01 | zero dólares e um centavo |
| 45 100 | quarenta e cinco mil e cem dólares |
| 1 000 000 | um milhão de dólares |
| 999 999 999,99 | novecentos e noventa e nove milhões novecentos e noventa e nove mil novecentos e noventa e nove dólares e noventa e nove centavos |

## Rules

- **"e" inside a group**, between hundreds, tens and units: "cento e vinte e cinco".
- **"e" between groups only before the last one, and only when that last group is below
  one hundred or a round hundred**: "mil e cem", "mil e um", "um milhão e quinhentos mil",
  but "mil duzentos e cinquenta". This is the rule taught in Brazilian grammars; it is what
  makes 45 100 read "quarenta e cinco mil e cem".
- **"cem" for exactly 100, "cento" before anything else**: "cem", "cento e um".
- **"mil", never "um mil"**; but "um milhão", "dois milhões".
- **"de" after an exact million**: "um milhão de dólares", "dois milhões de dólares", but
  "um milhão e cem dólares". The noun "milhão" needs the preposition when it is the last
  word before the currency.
- **Zero is plural**: "zero dólares", which is the usual Brazilian form.
- Dollars and cents are masculine nouns (o dólar, o centavo), so the masculine numerals apply
  throughout ("um", "dois", "duzentos").
- **No commas between groups.** Writing "novecentos e noventa e nove milhões, novecentos…"
  is also correct; the shorter form mirrors the English examples of the task.
- **"catorze"** is used for 14. "quatorze" is equally correct in Brazil; the official
  vocabulary (VOLP) lists both.

The sentence structure follows the task statement ("zero dólares e um centavo") instead of
the more idiomatic "um centavo de dólar", so every language answers the same question in
the same shape.
