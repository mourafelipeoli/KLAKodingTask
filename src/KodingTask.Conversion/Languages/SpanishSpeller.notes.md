| Amount | Output |
|---|---|
| 1 | un dólar |
| 21 | veintiún dólares |
| 25,1 | veinticinco dólares con diez centavos |
| 0,01 | cero dólares con un centavo |
| 45 100 | cuarenta y cinco mil cien dólares |
| 1 000 000 | un millón de dólares |
| 999 999 999,99 | novecientos noventa y nueve millones novecientos noventa y nueve mil novecientos noventa y nueve dólares con noventa y nueve centavos |

## Rules

- **"uno" is always shortened**: in an amount every number is followed by a noun
  ("dólares", "centavos") or by "mil" or "millones", where Spanish uses the apocopated form:
  "un dólar", "veintiún dólares", "treinta y un mil", "ciento un dólares".
- **One word up to twenty-nine**, with written accents where the stress requires them:
  "dieciséis", "veintidós", "veintitrés", "veintiséis", "veintiún". From thirty, tens and
  units are joined by "y": "cuarenta y cinco".
- **"cien" alone, "ciento" before more**: "cien dólares", "ciento un dólares", "cien mil".
  The hundreds have their own forms: "quinientos", "setecientos", "novecientos". They are
  masculine, agreeing with "dólares", "mil" and "millones".
- **"mil", never "un mil"**; "un millón", "dos millones"; "de" before the currency after an
  exact number of millions: "un millón de dólares".
- **Singular only for one**: "un dólar", "un centavo"; zero is plural ("cero dólares").
- **"con" between dollars and cents**, the usual connector when an amount of money is written
  out ("veinticinco dólares con diez centavos").
- **The cent is "centavo"**, the name of the hundredth of the dollar in every
  Spanish-speaking country that uses one; "céntimo" belongs to the euro.
